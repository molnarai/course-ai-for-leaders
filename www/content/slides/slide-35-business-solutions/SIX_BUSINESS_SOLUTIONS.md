# Six common agentic AI business solutions

These are six **widely deployed or frequently proposed solution patterns**, not a statistically ranked top six. The selection reflects McKinsey’s 2026 finding that agent scaling is most often reported in IT, knowledge management, and software engineering, with marketing/sales and supply chain prominent in particular industries; customer support is another established application. An *agentic* solution plans and executes multistep work using tools and organizational data, rather than merely answering a prompt. [web:8][web:11]

| Business solution | What it does | Expected business value | Agents and resources in a representative implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Customer-service resolution** | Triages incoming requests, retrieves account/order context, answers questions, performs permitted fixes (e.g., replacement or refund), and escalates exceptions. | Faster resolution and 24/7 coverage; lower cost per contact and improved customer retention, subject to quality controls. [web:10][web:11] | **Agents:** intake/intent, knowledge-and-policy, action/fulfillment, escalation/quality. **Resources:** chat/voice/email, CRM and ticketing APIs, order/billing systems, approved knowledge base, refund limits, human service team. |
| **Enterprise knowledge and research** | Searches internal material, synthesizes evidence, drafts briefings, and routes unresolved questions to subject-matter experts. | Less employee search time, faster decisions, and reuse of institutional knowledge; measure answer accuracy and time saved. [web:8][web:12] | **Agents:** query planner, retrieval/research, synthesis, citation/verification. **Resources:** permission-aware document index or graph, search/RAG, intranet and file connectors, LLM, access controls, expert review. |
| **Software engineering and modernization** | Turns requirements into code changes, writes tests and documentation, reviews changes, and assists with legacy migration under developer supervision. | Shorter delivery cycles, reduced maintenance effort, and potential build-versus-buy flexibility; measure accepted changes, defects, and lead time. [web:8][web:11] | **Agents:** planner, coding, test, review/security. **Resources:** Git repositories, issue tracker, CI/CD, test runners, sandbox, code-search index, developer approvals. |
| **IT service and incident operations** | Handles employee help requests, diagnoses alerts, proposes or executes runbook steps, and escalates risky incidents. | Lower mean time to resolve incidents, fewer repetitive tickets, and less downtime; measure resolution time and safe automation rate. [web:8][web:10] | **Agents:** triage, diagnostic, runbook executor, communications/escalation. **Resources:** service desk, logs/metrics/traces, CMDB, IAM, approved scripts and infrastructure APIs, on-call staff. |
| **Sales and marketing execution** | Qualifies leads, researches accounts, drafts personalized outreach, updates CRM, and tests campaign variants with approval gates. | Higher seller capacity, improved conversion and campaign effectiveness, and potential revenue growth; measure qualified pipeline and incremental conversion. [web:8][web:11] | **Agents:** prospect research, lead scoring, content/campaign, CRM follow-up, compliance review. **Resources:** CRM, product/pricing catalog, consent records, campaign platform, customer data, brand guidelines, sales approvals. |
| **Supply-chain and procurement orchestration** | Monitors inventory and supplier signals, forecasts exceptions, recommends replenishment or sourcing actions, and coordinates approvals and purchase orders. | Fewer stockouts and expediting costs, lower working capital, and shorter sourcing cycles; measure service level, inventory turns, and purchase-cycle time. [web:8][web:4] | **Agents:** demand/inventory monitor, supplier-risk researcher, sourcing optimizer, purchase-order executor. **Resources:** ERP, inventory and supplier records, contracts, demand forecasts, purchasing APIs, spending limits, buyer approval. |

## Design boundary

Agent roles above are **illustrative architectures**, not a claim that each application requires four separately deployed LLMs: one agent with several tools may suffice. Across all six, provide a model and orchestration layer, governed data access, least-privilege tool permissions, audit logs, evaluation/monitoring, and human approval for consequential actions. Expected value is a hypothesis to validate against a workflow baseline, not a guaranteed return. [web:11][web:12][web:8]
